# Idea Validation Checklist

Use this checklist to assess whether an idea is ready for validation research or if it needs more definition first.

---

## Idea Definition

- [ ] The idea is clearly articulated in 1-2 sentences
- [ ] The problem it solves is specific (not vague)
- [ ] We can name the target users (students, teachers, parents, etc.)
- [ ] We understand why this problem exists
- [ ] We've considered 2-3 alternative solutions

---

## Stakeholder Interest

- [ ] At least one potential user group has expressed interest
- [ ] A leader/decision-maker has shown openness to exploring this
- [ ] We've identified who would champion this if validated
- [ ] We've identified potential resisters and understand their concerns

---

## Assumptions

- [ ] We've identified the key assumptions (if we're wrong about these, the idea fails)
- [ ] We can articulate what we're uncertain about
- [ ] We know which assumptions are most critical to test first

**Critical assumptions checklist:**
- [ ] Users actually have this problem
- [ ] Users are motivated to solve it
- [ ] Our proposed solution is feasible
- [ ] The solution is better than current alternatives
- [ ] Implementation barriers are surmountable

---

## Preliminary Research

- [ ] We've done at least a quick literature search
- [ ] We know if competitors are doing something similar
- [ ] We've asked at least 3 potential users informally about this
- [ ] We understand the regulatory/policy context

---

## Scope & Scale

- [ ] We can pilot this idea (don't need all of organization to participate)
- [ ] We know the size of the potential impact (affects 50 students? 500?)
- [ ] We've identified an acceptable size for the pilot

---

## Timeline & Resources

- [ ] We could run a validation pilot in 2-4 weeks
- [ ] Someone has time to champion this validation work
- [ ] We have access to a small user group for testing
- [ ] We have budget for basic research (surveys, interviews, small pilot)

---

## Risk Assessment

- [ ] Implementation wouldn't disrupt ongoing work
- [ ] Failure of the pilot wouldn't be catastrophic
- [ ] We're comfortable with "we tried this and it didn't work"
- [ ] Legal/compliance risks are minimal

---

## Success Criteria

- [ ] We can articulate what "success" looks like for the pilot
- [ ] Success criteria are measurable, not just "people like it"
- [ ] We know what would make us decide "go" vs. "no go"

**Good criteria examples:**
- 60%+ of users engage with the tool weekly
- Feedback indicates it saves 30+ minutes/week
- Adoption rate reaches 40% in pilot phase

**Bad criteria examples:**
- "Teachers think it's useful"
- "Everyone will probably use it"
- "We believe in this"

---

## Scoring

**All items checked?** ✅ You're ready to plan validation research.

**Missing 1-2 items?** ⚠️ Spend a day or two filling gaps, then move forward.

**Missing 3+?** 🛑 The idea needs more definition. Spend time with stakeholders to clarify.

---

**Next Steps (if ready):**

1. Choose your validation method (surveys, interviews, pilot, etc.)
2. Use the hypothesis template to frame your research
3. Plan your timeline
4. Recruit participants
5. Run your validation
6. Analyze results
7. Make a go/no-go decision
